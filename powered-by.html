How to use it well

  • Only on sites we actually built or run. A credit on work we did is normal practice; a link on a site we had nothing to do with is not, and it's the thing that makes footer links look manipulative.
  • Keep the anchor branded. "Powered by CitedRealty" or "CitedRealty" — never keyword-stuffed anchor text like "best real estate marketing agency." Branded credits read as attribution; keyword anchors read as link building, and search engines treat them very differently.
  • One placement, in the footer. Don't repeat it across a page or inject it into body content.
  • Ask, don't assume. Put it in the build agreement or get a yes. A credit the client didn't agree to is a credit that gets ripped out later, and a conversation you'd rather not have.
  • Let them remove it. If a client asks it to come off, take it off. The relationship is worth more than the link.

What this is actually worth. Site-wide footer credits are usually discounted heavily by search engines, so treat this as a brand and referral play rather than a ranking one — real people click it, and consistent brand mentions across genuinely related sites are exactly the kind of signal AI assistants use when deciding who to name. It supports the backlink strategy; it isn't a substitute for one.
