Zillow Flex (Now Zillow Preferred): How It Works, What It Costs, Whether It's Worth It

CitedRealty · July 30, 2026 · For realtors & brokers

TL;DR — the short answer

Zillow Flex — which Zillow has been transitioning to the name Zillow Preferred — is the portal's invitation-only, pay-at-closing lead program: no upfront ad spend, and Zillow collects a success fee from your commission when a referred transaction closes. Independent guides report fees varying by market and price point, commonly cited in the roughly 15–40% range — at 35% on a $12,000 commission, that's $4,200. Invitations are performance-based (conversion, responsiveness, status updates, Follow Up Boss usage are all tracked), and falling below standards means losing the lead flow. The honest math: Flex is genuinely low-risk per deal, and at scale it's the most expensive marketing you'll ever love — a permanent commission tax on a pipeline you don't own and can lose with one performance dip. Use it as a supplement while building your own findability; program terms change, so verify current details with Zillow directly.

What Flex/Preferred actually is (and how it differs from Premier Agent)

Zillow sells agents leads two fundamentally different ways. Premier Agent is advertising: you pay upfront for a share of buyer inquiries in chosen ZIP codes, win or lose. Flex — the program Zillow has been transitioning to the name Zillow Preferred, with Flex partners rolled into it — flips the model: no upfront cost, and Zillow takes a success fee out of your commission when a referred deal closes. Zillow routes its connections (the people clicking Contact Agent and Request a Tour) to partner agents, tracks how they're served, and gets paid only on results. You can't buy your way in: access is invitation-only and performance-based, historically extended to strong Premier Agents and teams, with the newer "Zillow Pro" framework positioned as the path in going forward. Practically, it's a referral network wearing a portal costume — which is why the referral-fee economics and the pay-at-closing comparison are the right lenses for evaluating it.

The real cost: success fees and the math that matters

Zillow doesn't publish a national rate card, and terms vary by market, transaction price, and how the connection arrived. Independent guides consistently report success fees in a wide band — commonly cited around 15–40% of your side's gross commission, with illustrations in the 35% range: on a $12,000 commission, a 35% fee is $4,200 to Zillow. Treat every number here as reported-not-guaranteed and confirm your market's current terms with Zillow directly — the honest constant is the structure, not the rate.

The evaluation math is the same as any referral channel, with one addition. Per deal: your commission × (1 − fee) minus your cost to serve — usually a fine trade. At portfolio scale: the fee times your annual Flex-sourced volume is your real marketing budget for that pipeline — compare that number against what the same dollars would build in owned channels, and factor the hidden variables: connection quality varies (screened but not exclusive to intent), your conversion rate determines everything, and the service standards required to stay in the program (speed, follow-up cadence) are themselves a cost — one reason Flex teams often staff an ISA just to protect their metrics.

The performance treadmill (what staying in actually requires)

Flex/Preferred isn't a faucet you own; it's a slot you defend. Reported program standards include response-time expectations, conversion performance, mandatory status updates on every connection, closing-document submission, and required use of Follow Up Boss (Zillow's CRM) so the whole funnel is visible to Zillow. Under-perform the local cohort and lead flow shrinks or the invitation evaporates — the program is explicitly designed to concentrate connections on whoever converts them best. Two honest implications: the tracking discipline is genuinely good for most teams (forced follow-up hygiene improves every pipeline, not just Zillow's), and the dependency is real — an agent whose business is majority-Flex has handed a third party the ability to end their lead flow with a routing change. Ask anyone who's been through a portal algorithm shift what that feels like.

The disclosure context (worth knowing in 2026)

One more thing a Flex/Preferred agent should walk in knowing: the program's routing spent the last year in court. A consumer class action alleged that buyers clicking Contact Agent believed they were reaching the listing agent and weren't told about the success-fee routing; a federal judge dismissed the case in late July 2026, and no court has found the program unlawful — but the disclosure question is now public conversation (our full lawsuit explainer has the details). The practical move is simple and free: be proactively transparent with connections about how you met and how you're compensated. It defuses the exact suspicion the lawsuits planted, and it's the professional standard the post-settlement buyer-agreement conversation expects anyway.

Who should say yes, who should pass (the honest verdict)

Reasonable yes: teams with capacity and follow-up systems that can protect the metrics (Flex volume rewards machine-like response); newer-market expansions where you need deal flow before your own presence matures; anyone whose alternative is paying upfront for Premier Agent leads they convert poorly — pay-on-success beats pay-on-hope. Reasonable pass: solo agents whose capacity is already full (the fee buys volume you can't serve), anyone in a market where the success fee lands at the top of the band and margins are thin, and — most importantly — anyone using Flex as a substitute for building their own findability rather than a bridge while they do. The structural truth doesn't change with the branding: Flex is renting a pipeline at a premium, and rented pipelines are fine as a supplement. The agents who regret it are the ones who let the rented pipeline become the business — the full argument is in Zillow leads vs. owning your pipeline, and the alternative is the owned-visibility work this whole site is about. Program details change; verify current terms, fees, and requirements with Zillow before deciding.

FAQ

What is Zillow Flex and how does it work?

It's Zillow's invitation-only, pay-at-closing lead program — recently transitioning to the name Zillow Preferred. Instead of buying ads upfront (the Premier Agent model), partner agents receive Zillow's buyer and seller connections free and pay Zillow a success fee out of their commission when a referred transaction closes. Zillow tracks response times, conversion, and follow-up (Follow Up Boss use is required), and routes more connections to agents who perform. Terms vary by market; confirm current details with Zillow.

How much is the Zillow Flex referral fee?

Zillow doesn't publish a rate card; fees vary by market, price point, and connection type. Independent guides report success fees commonly in the roughly 15–40% range of the agent's gross commission on the referred side — illustrative examples run around 35%, which on a $12,000 commission means $4,200 to Zillow. Treat all reported figures as unofficial and confirm your market's current terms directly; the structure (percentage of commission, paid only at closing) is the reliable constant.

How do I get invited to Zillow Flex or Zillow Preferred?

Access is performance-based and invitation-only — historically extended to high-performing Premier Agents and teams with strong conversion and response metrics, and Zillow has positioned its newer Zillow Pro framework as the go-forward path. What reliably helps: excellent responsiveness and conversion on any existing Zillow connections, complete profile and review depth, and team infrastructure that can meet service standards. There's no application shortcut; the program recruits from observed performance.

Is Zillow Flex worth it?

Per deal, usually — paying a success fee on a closing that wouldn't otherwise have happened is a fine trade. At scale, do the honest math: your annual Flex fees are a marketing budget, and the question is whether those dollars would build more in owned channels that compound and can't be revoked. Flex works best as a supplement — capacity-filling volume while you build your own findability — and worst as the business itself, since the pipeline is rented, performance-gated, and can shrink with one routing change.

What's the difference between Zillow Premier Agent and Zillow Flex?

Payment model and access. Premier Agent is advertising: anyone can buy it, you pay upfront for connection share in chosen ZIPs, and the risk of non-converting leads is yours. Flex/Preferred is pay-at-closing: invitation-only, no upfront cost, and Zillow takes a success fee from closed referrals instead — with mandatory performance standards and CRM visibility in exchange. Same consumer traffic, opposite risk allocation; the fee band reported for Flex is the price of shifting that risk to Zillow.
